fin_tracker · trust & privacy

Three promises, written as facts.

Most finance apps write a trust page you have to take on faith. Every claim on this page is a fact about the shipped code — each states exactly what happens, and the sections below carry the payload, egress and who-can-see-it details, including the ones that make us look less clever.

Never sold. There are no ads, no trackers — and no billing at all: no payment processor, no plans, no metering. There is no revenue stream your data could substitute for.

Never used to train AI. Your data is never fed to model training — the product contains no training code. AI features send small, fixed-shape prompts to the endpoint you configure, and their exact contents are listed below.

Yours to take or delete. Settings → Privacy & data → Export my data (Download ZIP / Download JSON, plus a scheduled export) — and Settings → Account → Delete account (type DELETE + password) removes every row your account owns in one transaction.

Never sold

No ads, no data resale — and the honest part: nothing could be sold, because there is no billing anywhere in the product.

  • No ads and no tracking. Neither the app nor this site contains an ad, analytics or session-recording SDK. The site's own test scans every shipped page and fails the build if any page requests anything off-origin; the app loads no third-party scripts, fonts or images.
  • No billing, therefore no data revenue. No payment processor exists in the code — no plans, no metering, nothing to buy. A "never sold" promise is easy to keep when there is no ledger it could feed.
  • No bank credentials, ever. We never ask for your bank password and never sign in as you. Statements are files you choose to upload; bank sync exists only as an open-banking readiness seam, with no credential storage.
  • The outbound-HTTP inventory is public. The docs enumerate every destination the server can ever contact — all of them listed below — and none of them is an ad network, analytics vendor or data broker. Nothing else leaves.

Never used to train AI

State exactly what happens, no marketing fog.

  • There is no training pipeline. fin_tracker contains no model-training code — there is no place your data could be "used to train" from our side. Your data is never sent anywhere by us for training, fine-tuning or improvement.
  • AI features are inference-only, to the endpoint you configure. Categorization and import column-mapping each build a small, fixed-shape prompt and send it to the LLM base URL set in Settings → LLM provider — your own Ollama or your own OpenAI-compatible gateway, with your own key.
  • On the hosted instance, that endpoint is ours. The demo's AI runs against an Ollama server the operator runs on their own private network, serving an open-weight model from local weights (qwen2.5:7b‑instruct, verified live 2026‑11‑10). Your prompts do not reach OpenAI, Anthropic or any other model vendor.
  • Privacy mode seals it entirely. With privacy mode on, no AI request leaves unless the endpoint is on your own machine (loopback) — enforced at send time in one chokepoint, which refuses anything else with 403 privacy_mode_blocks_external_api.
  • The honest boundary. If you point the app at a third-party endpoint, that provider handles the request under its own terms — we don't control their side, and we won't pretend otherwise. What we control: what we put in the prompt (table below), where it goes (the URL you set), and that none of it is used to train anything by us.

Yours to take or delete

The actual Settings paths, not a description of a someday feature — both exist today and work with privacy mode on.

  • Export everything, any time. Settings → Privacy & data → Export my data: Download ZIP (one CSV per table plus a manifest) or Download JSON (every table, full fidelity). Per-account OFX downloads and a scheduled export that archives into your own browser's storage are in the app too. Pure database reads — they need no external service.
  • Delete everything, any time. Settings → Account → Delete account: confirm with your password and the word DELETE, and the server removes every row your account owns — accounts, transactions, staged imports, trades, budgets, rules, goals, shares, tokens — in one transaction, then the login itself (DELETE /api/v1/users/me). There is no "soft-delete grace period" quietly keeping a copy. The shared public demo is read-only by design, so this applies to accounts you create.

Where your data lives, and who can see it

  • Self-hosted (the design): your hardware. docker compose up on your server keeps the app and its Postgres database on your machine. Nothing syncs out of it beyond the destinations listed above.
  • The hosted instance: one server. The demo at fintrac.princessandmark.com is one server the operator controls (requests are TLS-terminated at the operator's own edge in front of it) — its public address geolocates to Toronto, Ontario, Canada (checked via IP geolocation 2026‑11‑10) — with one Postgres database in a container. No third-party cloud database, no data warehouse, no aggregator.
  • Who can see it: you; the operator of the instance you use (on the hosted demo, that's us — self-hosted, it's you); anyone you explicitly share a household link with; and, when you run an AI feature, the endpoint you configured. Nobody else — every table holding your data is filtered by your account in the application layer; Postgres row-level security ships as a second lock at the database and is active only on instances provisioned for it (the hosted instance currently runs without it; docs/55).
  • The honest status on encryption: not zero-knowledge. Client-side encryption is in progress — the server can still read your data, and the docs say exactly that. Your browser can keep an encrypted backup and scheduled export archives in its own storage; those never leave your device, and the server is the canonical store.
  • The door is guarded: TOTP two-factor and passkeys are built in, and agent access (MCP) works only through tokens you mint, scoped permissions and per-call receipts you can audit.

How these claims are checked

Trust copy that can't fail isn't trust copy.

  • The site's own test. A test scans every page the site ships — including this one — and fails the build if any page requests anything off-origin or carries an inline script.
  • Copy-drift pins. The Settings paths, the deletion endpoint and the AI payload numbers stated above are pinned against the actual source by a test — if the app changes and this page stops matching, the build goes red.
  • Accessibility. Every page, including this one, is scanned with axe-core at three viewport widths and must report zero violations.
